# Open decisions

Settle the first three at 12:30. The rest can wait until the loop runs.

## 1. Where does the board actually live

The brief says Lightfern. Nobody has written down what Lightfern can do for us, so whoever owns it should spend
ten minutes confirming it can hold a list of leads with a claim and a status, and say so before 13:00. If it
cannot, the fallback is a published page with a small shared store, which already works (see
`prototype-notes.md`).

## 2. Reddit and Hacker News only, or LinkedIn too

The brief puts LinkedIn out of scope for today, for good reasons: no free API, and scraping it is both against
their terms and technically hostile. A prototype built this morning does use LinkedIn, and it produced the
single best lead of the day, a person who sells candidate screening admin by hand to exactly our ICP.

**Recommendation: keep LinkedIn out of the build and show it in the Loom as the roadmap.** The free endpoints
are the honest demo, and judges can tell the difference between a thing that runs and a thing that needs a
logged-in browser.

## 3. Whose company is in the demo

Jumpstart, per the brief. This is the strongest choice we have and it should not be reopened: the judges watch
their own warm leads appear. It means the brain must be pointed at jumpstart's site, not ours, and the searches
must be about founders struggling to hire sales people, not recruiters drowning in screening notes.

## 4. What the scoring actually rewards

A post that describes the pain is not the same as a person who can buy. This morning's sweep found that most
people posting about a problem are vendors selling into it. Of nine results on one search, six were competitors.
So the disqualify rules matter as much as the score:

- Drop vendors and consultants selling the same solution.
- Drop candidates and job seekers; they feel the pain and hold no budget.
- Drop anyone outside the geography.
- Keep the dropped ones visible on screen with the reason, because showing the filter is what demonstrates
  judgement to a judge.

## 5. How many leads is enough for the demo

Ten good ones beats fifty unsorted. The board should look worked, not dumped.
