# Loom, 3 minutes

Pitched as if Jumpstart is a sales team deciding whether to buy. Value, not features. One person records it,
from about 15:00, with the demo already frozen.

## 0:00 to 0:30, the pain

Open on the real thing, not on the product. Your team spends hours researching prospects and the messages still
get ignored, because nothing in them proves you read anything. Meanwhile the buyer posted the problem in public
three hours ago and nobody was listening.

Say the line: stop guessing who is ready to buy, find the people already asking for you.

## 0:30 to 2:15, the demo

Point it at jumpstart.com. The brain reads the site and says back what Jumpstart sells and who buys it, in its
own words. Then the searches appear, written from the pain and not from job titles.

Real posts land, ranked. Open the top one: show the quote that qualified them, the intent score and the one
line saying why this person this week. Then the drafted reply, which quotes their own sentence back to them.

Claim it on the board. Say plainly that nothing sends itself, that a human reviews every message, and that this
is deliberate.

Show the filtered-out pile for ten seconds. Six of nine hits on one search were competitors selling the same
thing. The tool knows the difference. That is the moment a judge believes you have actually used it.

## 2:15 to 2:45, the value

Hours of research become minutes. Every message is relevant because the person asked for it. And the whole team
works one board, so nobody messages the same founder twice.

If a number exists by then, use a real one: posts swept, leads kept, how long the run took.

## 2:45 to 3:00, next

X, LinkedIn and a company news signal on funding, launches and hiring. One sentence each. Do not oversell the
roadmap; the judges are buying what they just watched.

## Rules

- Under three minutes. They stop watching at three.
- Never say AI-powered. Show the thing working instead.
- No feature lists. Each beat answers what it saves them.
